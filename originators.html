The development of HBCUs involved educators, donors, advocates, and community members who believed in the power of education.

RH

CHEYNEY UNIVERSITY

Richard Humphreys

Founder and benefactor

Richard Humphreys was a philanthropist whose financial bequest helped establish the institution now known as Cheyney University in 1837. His contribution supported education for Black students.

TT

FLORIDA A&M UNIVERSITY

Thomas DeSaille Tucker

First president of FAMU

Thomas DeSaille Tucker served as the first president of the State Normal College for Colored Students, which later became Florida A&M University. He helped guide the institution during its early years.

FLORIDA A&M UNIVERSITY

Thomas Van Renssalaer Gibbs

Early faculty member and educator

Thomas Van Renssalaer Gibbs was among the early educators associated with the founding of FAMU. His work contributed to the institution's early educational mission.

BW

TUSKEGEE UNIVERSITY

Booker T. Washington

First teacher and founding principal

Booker T. Washington became the first teacher and founding principal of Tuskegee Institute in 1881. He helped develop the school into an influential educational institution.

Read more →
MB

BETHUNE-COOKMAN UNIVERSITY

Mary McLeod Bethune

Educator and founder

Mary McLeod Bethune opened the Daytona Literary and Industrial Training School for Negro Girls in 1904. Beginning with five students, her school eventually developed into Bethune-Cookman University.
